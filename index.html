About Me

I'm a first-year Astronomy PhD student at UMass Amherst. As an astrophysicist at heart, I love studying the cosmos through numerical simulations. I'm currently working on feedback and feeding mechanisms for supermassive black holes. I'm very interested in Little Red Dots, galactic evolution, star formation, and dark matter.

I'm a first-generation Mexican American from San Diego, California. I took a very untraditional path. After a very lackluster high school, I attended San Diego Community College District as a computer science major. I switched to physics after taking Astronomy 101 to fulfill a general course requirement and to get away from coding (boy, was I about to find out soon). I then transferred to San Diego State University and graduated with distinction with a B.S. in Astronomy. Now I'm at UMass Amherst as a part of Yuan Li's group.

I love to work out, cook, watch anime, play video games, and hang out with friends.

Recent Works

Star Formation Efficiency in STARFORGE

Theory and observations often disagree on how efficiently giant molecular clouds turn gas into stars. I made mock far-infrared dust emission maps of STARFORGE simulations with radiative transfer, so the simulations could be measured the same way observers measure real clouds. Started at Carnegie Observatories and continued as my senior thesis at San Diego State. Published in ApJ with Dr. Mike Grudić and Dr. Anna Rosen.
